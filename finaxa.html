PRODUCT DESIGN / FINTECH / WEB + MOBILE

Finaxa

Make the next money move a more informed one.

Finaxa brings everyday banking into one experience. I designed the dashboard and flows for transfers, cards, loans, and reports, with an AI assistant concept that helps users explore information before deciding what to do.

My Role UI/UX Designer
Platforms Web + mobile
Scope Banking flows & design system
Evidence Design work · Validation planned
01 / THE PROBLEM

Fast to find. Clear enough to verify.

Customers need a quick overview, but banking decisions also require detail. The challenge was to make common actions discoverable without hiding the information needed to review a transfer, card transaction, or statement.

Accounts Balances & activity
Transfers Payees & methods
Cards & loans Servicing workflows

02 / THE USER NEED

Support the quick task and the bigger picture.

I designed for two needs: getting an everyday banking task done and understanding where money goes over time. The information hierarchy has to accommodate both without giving every feature equal weight.

User Type 1
A

Everyday banking

  • – Checks balances and recent activity
  • – Uses quick actions to transfer money or pay bills
  • – Needs to verify the payee and method
  • – Looks for an appropriate transfer method and limit
User Type 2
F

Managing finances

  • – Tracks spending, income, and ongoing commitments
  • – Reviews card, loan, and subscription details
  • – Uses reports to understand money movement
  • – Downloads statements when a record is needed

05 / A CRITICAL JOURNEY

Separate choosing a payee from confirming a transfer.

The flow starts with a saved payee, then presents the transfer details for review. The design intent is to make the commitment point explicit instead of treating every step as another form field.

07 / REVIEW BEFORE ACTION

The details belong before the commitment.

Transfer, card, and loan screens surface the information people need to inspect before proceeding: methods and limits, transaction details, and installment terms. This is a design intention, not a tested trust outcome.

08 / SYSTEMS THINKING

Use the system to make complex screens coherent.

Shared colors, Hanken Grotesk typography, and spacing support the same banking language across desktop and mobile. Reusable components make recurring account and transaction patterns easier to maintain.

Scroll horizontally to view typography
09 / THE AI CONCEPT

A conversational entry point—not an invisible decision-maker.

The assistant concept helps people explore spending, look up activity, and find banking information. The conversation complements the core task flows rather than replacing the review steps shown in the product.

10 / CROSS-PLATFORM CONTINUITY

Start with an insight. Continue with the details.

A customer can review spending on mobile, then inspect recurring payments in the desktop card workspace. The two surfaces support different levels of detail within the same financial task.

11 / EXPLAINABLE OPTIONS

Make the comparison visible in the conversation.

The savings-account concept presents three options side by side before the customer asks for details. Showing the alternatives makes the choice inspectable instead of leaving the recommendation unexplained.

12 / REFLECTION & NEXT TEST

Clarity is the goal. Usability still needs evidence.

The work delivers desktop and mobile banking flows plus a reusable UI system. Next I would test whether people can explain a transfer limit before proceeding and find the right report without assistance.
